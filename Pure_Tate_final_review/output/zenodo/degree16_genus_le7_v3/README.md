# Degree-sixteen cohomology in genus at most seven

This directory contains the third revised source package for the manuscript
“Degree-sixteen cohomology of moduli spaces of stable curves in genus at
most seven is Tate,” dated 2 September 2026.  The preceding version is
preserved in the sibling directory `degree16_genus_le7_v2`, which remains
byte-identical to the copy hash-pinned by the Lean campaigns `LG7D16-004`,
`LG7D16-006`, and `LG7D16-007`.

## Changes relative to v2 (adversarial-review pass, 2 September 2026)

All changes are expository or bibliographic; no mathematical step of the v2
argument was altered.  Every numbered citation was checked against the cited
source.

1. Looijenga's vanishing on `M_g` is his Theorem 1.2, not Theorem 1.1
   (Theorem 1.1 of that paper concerns fiber powers of the universal
   curve).  The citation in the Ionel–Looijenga proposition is corrected.
2. The Canning–Larson–Payne primitive-quotient formula (their Lemma 3.1(a))
   is stated in the source as an isomorphism of Q-vector spaces.  The
   proposition quoting it no longer claims an isomorphism of pure Hodge
   structures, and an explicit sentence records that only the vanishing
   consequence is used.
3. The virtual-cohomological-dimension lemma now cites Brown, Chapters VII
   and VIII (Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence and Serre's theorem/vcd),
   instead of Chapter VII alone, and names Serre's theorem for the
   dimension of the chosen finite-index subgroup.
4. On the critical line the equality `k = 4g-4+n` is now labelled
   `vcd(PMod_{g,n})`, matching the notation of the Harer citation, with the
   positivity of `n` recorded.
5. The convention that the tautological ring of the open moduli stack is
   the restriction of the tautological ring of the compactification
   (hence generated by psi- and kappa-classes) is now stated once, where
   `R^*` is introduced, and the Ionel argument refers back to it.
6. A remark now situates Liu's preprint arXiv:2509.02950 (tautological Chow
   rings and CKgP for `M_{5,8}` and `M_{5,9}`): those announced results
   would give the `(5,8)` row by the direct-range mechanism, but the
   argument here is independent of that unrefereed preprint.
7. The genus-eight reduction records why no genus-eight vertex occurs in
   the one-edge boundary of `(8,0)`, and Wong's theorem is quoted with its
   hypothesis `g >= 1`.
8. Small precision edits: the direct-range bookkeeping in the open-term
   proposition distinguishes `g = 4` (where `(4,10)` itself lies in the
   published range); the page-count field was dropped from the Forum of
   Mathematics Pi entry; the Payne survey and Liu entries are marked as
   preprints.

## Mathematical scope

The unconditional theorem proved in the manuscript states that, for every
stable pair `(g,n)` with `g <= 7`,

`H^16(overline M_{g,n}; Q)`

is a direct sum of copies of `Q(-8)` as a polarizable rational Hodge
structure.  The critical open case `M_{5,8}` is handled directly by the
same one-marking argument as the other exceptional endpoints: the
Canning--Larson--Payne primitive quotient, virtual cohomological dimension,
their published `n=7=c(5)` Chow--Kunneth range, and Ionel's tautological
vanishing.  This version has no dependence on Liu's preprint (cited only in
a remark as related work) and does not require a separate genus-five
appendix.

The global all-genera statement is not claimed unconditionally.  The paper
proves that it follows from the two open genus-eight inputs for `(8,0)` and
`(8,1)`.  The apparent case `(8,2)` vanishes by Wong's Theorem 4.1.

The manuscript determines Hodge type only.  It does not compute an exact
Betti number and does not claim algebraic or tautological generation of the
compact degree-sixteen cohomology.
